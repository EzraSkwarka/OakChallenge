1. The Basics


   1. What is a Professor Oak Challenge?


Professor Oak has always had a dream to fill the pokedex, but he’s too old to do it! It’s up to you as a new trainer to complete that pokedex as much as you can independently by catching, evolving and breeding as many pokemon as possible. Professor Oak also isn’t getting any younger, so you need to complete that dex as a priority! Maybe you should put your gym challenge on hold for a while…


In 2018, a Reddit user named Chamale posted updates of a unique challenge on Pokemon Crystal which at the time didn’t have a name but was referred to as ‘Oak Mode’ or a ‘Professor Oak Run/Challenge’. I was really intrigued by this style of gameplay and set out to play this on FireRed, writing a guide as I went along. After the success of the run I tried it on different versions and have now played the majority of the main series games and even a couple of spin offs. 


The basic concept of the challenge follows these simple rules:
* You have to catch and evolve (or breed) as many pokemon as is possible in the game until you can’t proceed any further until a gym badge is required e.g. needing a badge to be able to use a HM move or in most cases, to allow you to proceed with the plot or to get NPCs to move out of the way. For example, you will need to fully evolve your starter amongst others before defeating the Santalune Gym for your first badge. For parts where gyms can be done in any order, always get the badge that unlocks the most pokemon.
* You cannot trade with other versions. This means that trade evolutions are off the table, including GTS trades. This means you’ll get no version exclusives from the other version and you’re stuck with the decisions you make such as your starter or fossil decisions. In-game trades are allowed as that NPC is in your version of the game.
* No glitches are allowed


   2. Tips on this version


* Grinding – The first section of a Professor Oak Challenge is always very daunting as you need to get your starter fully evolved. The horrible exp mechanics from Generation 5 are no longer present (but return in Generation 7!) so the exp you gain is determined solely by the level and species of the pokemon you’ve defeated. There is no way to rematch trainers (in the conventional sense) in X and Y as there is no VS Seeker. There are several places with trainers that refresh periodically (covered later). 


* Experience boosts – New to Generation 6, there’s a few extra ways to boost exp gain. The first is Pokemon Amie (accessed on the lower screen). Interacting with your pokemon here by feeding them pokepuffs and stroking/petting them with them will boost their affection levels (measured by pink hearts when you switch between pokemon). Getting a pokemon to two affection hearts will give it a 20% exp bonus so naturally you’ll be wanting to do this to every pokemon you’re planning on levelling up. If you stroke the pokemon until it stops giving out hearts, fill it with as many pokepuffs as you have or it can eat, then play a game. I prefer the tile puzzles as they’re over very quickly. Return to your pokemon and interact with it again for more hearts, feed it the pokepuffs you’ve received from the game and then repeat this process until you get the two affection hearts which you’ll be notified of when your pokemon does an appreciation dance with lots of hearts coming from it. The second method is preventing your pokemon from evolving through levelling up. If a pokemon has reached the level in which it evolves (e.g. Caterpie reaches level 7), if you keep it at that unevolved form, it will gain a 20% boost which stacks with the Pokemon Amie boost. This is obviously only useful for three stage pokemon (Starters, Pidgey, Caterpie, Fletchling etc) but is very useful as many of these 3 stage pokemon evolve into their final stage quite late. In cases like these, keep your pokemon unevolved until it reaches the level before its final evolution. For example, Fletchinder evolves into Talonflame at Level 35 so if you keep Fletchling unevolved until it’s Level 34, let it evolve, and then it’s only one level without the exp gain to get it to evolve into Talonflame. Finally there’s the exp O-Power that Mr Bonding will give you. This isn’t until quite late in the game but it’s very handy. The more you use it, the quicker it will level up to give even bigger boosts in exp gained. 


* Super Training – There’s two reasons to use this. One is that you can use it to obtain evolution stones as early as prior to the first badge. In order to access these, you will need a pokemon with maximum EVs (indicated by the bar on the right). There is no guarantee that you will win these stones, you may have to repeat the course many times. As long as you complete it within the target time, you’ll have a chance of winning the item. Secondly, you can use super training to specifically focus on boosting a stat. For example, my Burmy wasn’t doing well in battle so I buffed its attack stat up through training.


* Critical captures – From as early as having 30 pokemon registered as caught in your dex, you will start having a small chance of a critical capture which significantly increases your chance of capturing a pokemon. Given the nature of this challenge, you will find this very beneficial as you rack up the amount of captures.


* Learning new moves shouldn’t be taken for granted, particularly with pokemon that evolve at higher levels. During a normal game you might be quick to replace a weak move with a stronger one but getting rid of non-attacking moves is a better strategy here. On the first part of the challenge before the Santalune Gym, you are battling low level pokemon and will run out of PP very quickly while that exp bar goes up very slowly. You need to maximize the amount of pokemon you can defeat, so having multiple attacking moves, regardless of how weak they are, will be beneficial. You have no need for moves like Growl or Sand Attack. Another advantage other than the exp boost above is that if you keep your three stage pokemon unevolved they will learn moves faster than their middle stage would.


* This challenge is a fun alternative to a regular play through and you may really appreciate having to train pokemon up yourself that you never trained before. On the other hand you may end up despising certain pokemon due to their inability to attack when you first get them or their generally poor stats/levelling up moves. 


* Please note that this is not a full guide to the game, I am only listing the pokemon you can catch at the earliest opportunities and any required items. Please consult other guides for a full walkthrough. This guide assumes you know how to get through the caves etc throughout the game.


* And finally, remember that this is YOUR challenge. There are things you can do to make this run easier or harder and it’s entirely at your discretion how you want to handle this. A lot of people like to use pokemon with the Pickup ability (e.g. Zigzagoon) to farm Rare Candies to level up your pokemon. You could use exp share, you could put pokemon in the day-care and you could switch train pokemon (send it out and then replace with a better pokemon). I’m quite hard core/sadistic with these challenges and don’t like using any of the above methods, I only like gaining experience through battling to make this a real challenge!

This first part will cover everything you can do from the get-go right up to the Santalune City Gym BEFORE you get that first badge from Viola. This WILL take a while so it will really test your patience…


Vaniville and Aquacorde Town - 
Get dressed and leave your house and your new neighbours will meet you. They’ll inform you that there is a meeting in Aquacorde Town where you can get your first pokemon. Travel straight there through Route 1 (why they made this an actual route I don’t know…) and you’ll bump into the group of friends who welcome you to join them. Now it’s time to pick your starter! There’s no real advantages over type or evolution level, it’s very much your preference. By the time it’s fully evolved, type advantages won’t matter against the first gym leader! Once you’ve picked, head home to see your mother and you’ll get challenged by Shauna who has the starter that’s weak to yours, making this an easy battle. Once you’ve told your mother that you’re leaving, head back to Aquacorde Town. There’s a free healing spot in one of the shops and you can also spend all your money on pokeballs as you’ll be needing them! 


* Chespin--(Lv. 16)-->Quilladin--(Lv. 36)-->Chesnaught
OR
* Fennekin--(Lv. 16)-->Braixen--(Lv. 36)-->Delphox
OR
* Froakie--(Lv. 16)-->Frogadier--(Lv. 36)-->Greninja


Route 2 (Avance Trail) – 
Go north from Aquacorde and you’ll see a patch of grass straight away. Oddly enough the capture tutorial is by the second patch of grass so you can show your rival who’s boss by catching everything before they give you the tutorial. Bear in mind that there’s no pokemon centre until you pass through Santalune Forest so you’ll be limited to the six pokemon in your party. Your first encounter will always be a Pidgey. Other than that, you can run around to flush out Bunnelby, Fletchling, Scatterbug, and Zigzagoon. You’ll also find Weedle in X and Caterpie in Y but you’ll be able to catch the other in the forest. 


* Pidgey--(Lv. 18)-->Pidgeotto--(Lv. 36)-->Pidgeot
* Bunnelby--(Lv. 20)-->Diggersby
* Fletchling--(Lv. 17)-->Fletchinder--(Lv. 35)-->Talonflame
* Scatterbug--(Lv. 9)-->Spewpa--(Lv. 12)-->Vivillon
* Zigzagoon--(Lv. 20)-->Linoone
* (X) Weedle--(Lv. 7)-->Kakuna--(Lv. 10)-->Beedrill
* (Y) Caterpie--(Lv. 7)-->Metapod--(Lv. 10)-->Butterfree


Both Bunnelby and Zigzagoon can have the ability Pickup so take advantage of this if you want a Pickup squad. They also keep this ability once evolved. 


Santalune Forest – 
Notice anything familiar? This is almost identical to Viridian Forest from Kanto. In here you’ll team up with Shauna who you can talk to and she’ll heal your team up. This is very helpful so consider sticking around for training. You’ll be able to pick up the other bug you didn’t find on Route 2 (Caterpie/Weedle), a Pikachu (catch a female for breeding later on) and the three elemental monkeys – Pansage, Pansear and Panpour. 


* (X) Caterpie--(Lv. 7)-->Metapod--(Lv. 10)-->Butterfree
* (Y) Weedle--(Lv. 7)-->Kakuna--(Lv. 10)-->Beedrill
* Pikachu
* Pansage
* Pansear
* Panpour


Route 3 (Ouvert Way) – 
This next route isn’t a long one but there’s several young trainers here and of course some new pokemon. Consider skipping ahead to Santalune City (home of the first gym) to access the pokemon centre and change your party should you want to. On Route 3 you’ll be able to catch a Bidoof, Azurill, Burmy and Dunsparce. Dunsparce is also available on the next route. You will need to catch two Burmy, one male and one female, due to the different evolutions. Once you’ve caught Azurill, keep it in your team so that the extra steps from running around raise its happiness. You should also battle with it a few times early on to get the happiness boosts from levelling up. Since Marill evolves into Azumarill at Level 18, you want to try and get Azurill to evolve at Level 17 at the very latest. Save your game before levelling up and if it still hasn’t evolved, keep it in your party and evolve it later on once its happiness has increased. Another way you could raise its friendship is if you are really lucky and obtain a Soothing Bag as part of the Super Training punching bags. 


* Bidoof--(Lv. 15)-->Bibarel
* Burmy(female) --(Lv. 20)-->Wormadam
* Burmy(male) --(Lv. 20)-->Mothim
* Azurill--(Happiness)-->Marill--(Lv. 18)-->Azumarill
* Dunsparce


Route 22 (Detourner Way) – 
Go east out of Santalune City to access this route that eventually leads to a blocked off gate. We won’t pass through this until the end of the game. On this route however we can battle some more trainers and catch some more pokemon. You can catch Farfetch’d, Litleo, Riolu and Psyduck. Farfetch’d is actually available to trade for in Santalune City for a Bunnelby but since it’s here you may as well just catch it yourself. Try to avoid catching a Litleo with Rivalry as the stat drop against the opposite gender isn’t helpful. Catch one with Unnerve (it activates when entering battle). Riolu is another happiness evolution pokemon so again, keep it in your party and level it up a few times early on. Note that it only evolves during the day. 


* Farfetch’d
* Litleo--(Lv. 35)-->Pyroar
* Riolu--(Happiness daytime)-->Lucario
* Psyduck--(Lv. 33)-->Golduck


And with that we’ve hit the wall already! We need to fully evolve everything before we tackle the gym leader. 


Secret Super Training for Stones – 
Complete the Super Training courses with a pokemon of your choice, preferably a late evolver such as your starter. This also helps to max out your EVs (the bar on the right of the Super Training screen). Once your EVs are maxed out, you’ll have access to some additional courses that reward evolution stones. You will need to clear the course in the targeted time but there’s no guarantee that you’ll win the stone. For this section you’ll need a Leaf, Fire, Water and Thunder Stone meaning that you’ll need to win the prizes from the courses “The Leaf Stone Cup Begins! (Venusaur)”, “The Fire Stone Cup Begins! (Charizard)”, “The Water Stone Cup Begins! (Blastoise)” and “Follow Those Fleeing Goals! (Stunfisk)” respectively. Alternatively you can try the final course “The Battle For The Best!” where either of the four stones is given out as a prize.


* Pansage--(Leaf Stone)-->Simisage
* Pansear--(Fire Stone)-->Simisear
* Panpour--(Water Stone)-->Simipour
* Pikachu--(Thunder Stone)-->Raichu


Levelling up tips – 
Definitely take advantage of the experience boost from Pokemon Amie and keep your three stage pokemon unevolved as long as possible (one level before the middle stage would evolve into the final stage). Route 22 is absolutely the best place to train at this point. With a combined total of a 15% encounter rate, Dunsparce and Farfetch’d give very nice boosts. Other than that, it’s just a very simple grind to get your pokemon to evolve.

Hoo boy, this is a big section. It’s quite a trek to get to the next gym so naturally we open up many new areas and can even advance a fair but further than the gym itself. One thing I love about X and Y is that very few routes repeat the same pokemon or just have evolutions of earlier pokemon. Every route has lots of new pokemon to catch. Leave Santalune City to the north and speak to Alexa who will give you the Exp Share. It’s up to you whether you permit this item in your challenge. All pokemon in your party get exp regardless of whether they participated or not. If you choose not to use this (like me), you will have to go to your key items to switch it off as it is switched on by default. 


Route 4 (Parterre Way) – 
There are a nice collection of trainers here to practice against but obviously you’ll want to catch some new pokemon first. On this route you’ll be able to find Budew, Flabébé, Ledyba, Skitty, Combee and Ralts. Budew is a happiness evolution (during the day). Feel free to catch it now and keep it in your party to raise its happiness or come back after Lumiose City where you’ll be given some Luxury Balls which will give Budew a higher base happiness making it easier to evolve. Roselia is also found later on in this section on Route 7 if you want to skip the evolution. You’ll also need a female Combee to get it to evolve into Vespiquen. Combee is more common in the yellow flowers than the red ones. Finally you’ll also need to catch TWO Ralts, one specifically being a male to get Gallade. You can get the Moon, Shiny and Dawn Stones from Secret Super Training regimens: “Drag Down Hydreigon!”, “An Opening of Lightning Quick Attacks! (Roserade)” and “Those Long Shots Are No Long Shot! (Gallade)” respectively. 


* Budew--(Happiness daytime)-->Roselia--(Shiny Stone)-->Roserade
* Flabébé--(Lv. 19)-->Floette--(Shiny Stone)-->Florges
* Ledyba--(Lv. 18)-->Ledian
* Skitty--(Moon Stone)-->Delcatty
* Combee female--(Lv. 21)-->Vespiquen
* Ralts--(Lv. 20)-->Kirlia--(Lv. 30)-->Gardevoir
* Ralts male--(Lv. 20)-->Kirlia male--(Dawn Stone)-->Gallade


Lumiose City – 
Once you’re finished up on Route 4, head north to meet Dexio and Sina who will urge you to visit Professor Sycamore. You’ll only be able to access some of Lumiose due to a black out, meaning that many roads are currently blocked off. Head straight to Sycamore’s lab to get the ball rolling. Talk to one of his assistants on the second floor for the aforementioned Luxury Balls. Visit Sycamore on the third floor where he’ll battle you with the three Kanto starters and will then ask you to pick one to take with you. For those looking for the quickest pokemon to evolve, Bulbasaur evolves into its final stage earlier than the others. If you don’t mind about that, pick your favourite. You’ll then be given the relevant mega stone for its final evolution which we can’t use yet anyway. 


* Bulbasaur--(Lv. 16)-->Ivysaur--(Lv. 32)-->Venusaur
OR
* Charmander--(Lv. 16)-->Charmeleon--(Lv. 36)-->Charizard
OR
* Squirtle--(Lv. 16)-->Wartortle--(Lv. 36)-->Blastoise


Take the time to visit the areas within Lumiose. You can get the Lens Case from the PR studio and get your haircut so that you can customise your trainer a little better. There’s also Café Le Nah where you can battle for some extra exp as well as earn some expensive items to sell at the Pokemart. You’ll also be able to pick up some Timer and Quick Balls and the Stone Emporium can also be visited which sells Leaf, Fire and Water Stone if you get frustrated trying to win them in Super Training. Visit your rival at the café near the exit to Route 5 and then carry on with your journey.


Route 5 (Versant Road) – 
Tierno and Trevor will be waiting up ahead but feel free to go at your own pace. This is the first route in which you can engage in a horde battle – five pokemon (lower levelled) will appear at once. This is the only way to encounter Scraggy and depending on your version, you’ll be able to find Plusle/Minun through standard encounters with the other being potentially available in a rare horde battle. For example, Plusle can be found in X, but Minun can only be found if there’s a horde battle with four Plusle and one Minun. Horde battles can activate randomly whilst running around in the grass but you can also trigger them by using Honey in the item bag or using the move Sweet Scent in the field (Combee is a good candidate). Aside from these three horde pokemon, you can expect to encounter Gulpin, Abra, Pancham, Doduo, Skiddo and Furfrou. Kadabra is the first trade evolution pokemon of the challenge so we are unfortunately not able to obtain it as we can’t trade pokemon.


* Scraggy--(Lv. 39)-->Scrafty
* Plusle
* Minun

Camphrier Town and the Road Block – 
Visit Camphrier Town and rest up. There’s not a whole lot to do at present. Head out west and you can visit the berry fields to the south. Take advantage of these fields if you want more berries. Occasionally while the berry tree is growing, a wild pokemon may appear when you investigate a shaking tree. This is a nice way to catch Volbeat and Illumise but we can catch them more consistently later on so I won’t list them here. We’ll soon come across a Snorlax who stops us from accessing Route 7 proper so we will need to take a detour past Route 6 (Palais Lane) to get to Parfum Palace for a pokeflute. Running up the path on Route 6, the larger bushes may shake and a wild pokemon will jump up. Most of the time this will be a Venipede but if you’re lucky an Audino will appear. If you’re familiar with generation 5 games, you’ll know that Audino gives a boat load of experience. Reach the end of the path and you can make a U-turn to the left or right to run around in the tall grass. Wild pokemon here include Espurr, Kecleon, Honedge, Oddish, Sentret and Nincada. You’ll need to catch two Oddish here for both of Gloom’s evolutions. To get a Sun Stone you can also receive this from the Secret Super Training Course “Drag Down Hydreigon!” while the Dusk Stone which is the last stone of the set can be obtained from the course “Watch Out! That’s One Tricky Second Half! (Aegislash)”. As always with Shedinja, make sure that when your Nincada evolves that you have a spare slot on your team otherwise Shedinja will disappear. Grab the Cut HM from the gardens of Parfum Palace and then corner Furfrou and you’ll get the pokeflute. Also pick up the Amulet Coin indoors for some serious money making coming up.

Route 7 (Riviere Walk) – 
Head back to Snorlax and SAVE THE GAME before talking to the people on the bridge. You also want to make sure that the Snorlax is a female. Though we can’t get the Incense to breed for a Munchlax yet, this will occur later on before we can even catch a Ditto and I’ve seen many players stumble on this. With Snorlax gone we can proceed down Route 7. The day-care centre is our next stop where you can start doing some breeding if you fancy it. There’s only one pokemon to breed for our dex at this point. Put a female Pikachu/Raichu in with an appropriate partner (e.g. Linoone) to get a Pichu. In the grass of the route we can catch Smeargle, Volbeat, Illumise, Croagunk and Ducklett. Our first version exclusives are on this route – Swirlix in X and Spritzee in Y. Both evolutions are trade evolutions, therefore we can’t get them. You will also need to do a horde battle to find Hoppip. If you haven’t evolved Budew yet, Roselia can be caught here. 


* Snorlax (female)
* Pikachu/Raichu--(Breed)-->Pichu

The Battle Chateau – 
Now this is a great area to train in. When you first enter you’ll be given the title of Baron/Baroness. As you defeat more and more trainers here, your rank may increase to Viscount/Viscountess, Earl/Countess, Marquis/Marchioness, Duke/Duchess and finally Grand Duke/Grand Duchess. With each rank increase, stronger trainers will appear. It’s possible to push your rank all the way up to Grand Duke/Grand Duchess at this point in the game. Bulbapedia seems to be slightly incorrect in that it says you cannot advance to the Grand titles without beating all trainers INCLUDING gym leaders and Elite Four. However I did this with only Viola making an appearance, so perhaps it means defeating any gym leaders that CAN appear there at your current point in the game. With this level increase in mind, always make sure you have higher levelled pokemon such as late evolvers in case your weaker pokemon can’t handle the level difference since pokemon can range up to Lv50. Trainers replenish every six minutes or even faster if you pay for the various writs which last until the end of the day. The most useful one is the Silver Writ of Challenge which brings in a new trainer every minute! With the Amulet Coin equipped you can rake in some serious money as well as exp. Your opponents may occasionally give you an item to sell for lots of money e.g. Nuggets. As your rank increases you can even activate writs that increase the level of your opponents making this the ideal place to train. Once you reach Marquis/Marchioness, trainers with Audino start to appear (easily identified, they are wearing Kimono style clothing) which makes for even more exp! Suffice to say, you’ll be here a LOT, especially once you’ve caught everything in this section. If you’re enabling Exp Share then you can seriously fill up your dex quickly in here by battling with the same pokemon and letting everyone else in your team get half the exp with a high chance of reaching Lv100 before the end of this section. You can even activate certain writs that increase the level of the opponents!


Connecting Cave – 
There’s two cave entrances on Route 7, the northern one is a dead end until you have Strength, the southern one is the way forwards. The southern cave also has someone who will heal your pokemon. In the cave, catch a Zubat, Meditite, Whismur and Axew before leaving out the other side. Consider catching Zubat in a Luxury Ball so that it will likely evolve into Crobat as soon as it’s evolved into Golbat. 


* Zubat--(Lv. 22)-->Golbat--(Happiness)-->Crobat
* Meditite--(Lv. 37)-->Medicham
* Whismur--(Lv. 20)-->Loudred--(Lv. 40)-->Exploud
* Axew--(Lv. 38)-->Fraxure--(Lv. 48)-->Haxorus


Route 8 (Muraille Coast) – 
Upon entering the route, Dexio and Sina will stop you and upgrade your pokedex to include the Coastal Dex. Pretty much all the pokemon we catch from here will be registered in that section. One thing to be careful about in this area is the possibility of a sky battle. If you have a pokemon that flies (e.g. its animation is always airborne) then you may be drawn into a battle with a sky trainer using ONLY those pokemon that fly. This can be bad news if you’re training a weak pokemon and you’re stuck using it in one of these battles. Thankfully these are all optional and you get the choice of participating, so if you don’t have a decent pokemon for this, decline the battle! Grab the free Leaf Stone here.


Standard encounters here will let you find Spoink, Absol, Drifloon, Bagon, Mienfoo, Inkay and either Zangoose (X) or Seviper (Y). In the horde battles you’ll be able to find Wingull, Taillow and the other exclusive (Seviper/Zangoose). Skiddo is a fantastic candidate as it learns both of these moves. Another alternative is to use a pokemon with Intimidate so that the four pokemon don’t have enough power to knock out the pokemon you want fast enough. You could catch it normally depending on which moves the four pokemon use and, in very rare instances, they may attack you instead of their rival.

Glittering Cave – 
This place is very interesting in terms of wild encounters. In the first room you’ll be in a 3D maze. You’ll need to approach the weird black blobs that appear throughout the tunnel. These will trigger an encounter with a pokemon. Here you’ll be able to find a large number of pokemon: Kangaskhan, Machop, Onix, Solrock, Lunatone, Rhyhorn, Cubone and Mawile. Once you’re inside the next room, you’ll have a few battles with Team Flare before rescuing the assistant who will let you pick between the Jaw Fossil (Tyrunt) or the Sail Fossil (Amaura). Once you’re done, focus on the encounters in the room. This will require you to walk under a shadowy spot on the floor which means that a pokemon will drop down and attack. You can simply leave and re-enter the room to get them to respawn. You’ll find a Woobat and a Ferroseed this way. Levelling up tips – 
You have A LOT of pokemon to train and you have multiple options available to you. If you are using the exp share, just train in the Battle Chateau with a strong pokemon (which may even reach Lv100) with a writ that speeds up trainer regeneration. Your party will get crazy exp, especially against the Audino trainers, so simply switch out party members once they’ve evolved. If you’re not using exp share, I used Hoppip horde battles (who have no attacking moves) to raise weak pokemon up to level 15, Seviper/Zangoose hordes from levels 15-25/30 and the Battle Chateau for pokemon over those levels. The few ghost types you get are entirely immune to Audino’s moves so feel free to sponge up that huge exp. A really cheap tactic I used against the Audino trainers was buying lots of Super Potions with my excess money, giving a pokemon the Rocky Helmet to hold and then I simply healed after every hit until Audino KO’d itself from recoil and Rocky Helmet damage.

Failing that, an alternative that will halt your progress for the rest of the section is to fully evolve Goomy before completing the mandatory events on Route 14 since the game is scripted to always rain until you’ve progressed far enough.

I always like to put these extra sections in my guides where the rules of a POC start to go out the window but there’s still pokemon you can catch in your game. Since there’s no trading involved it’s iffy, but you need to connect to another game/player. X and Y’s unique feature is the Friend Safari. It will take some dedication and hunting for players with specific pokemon to complete this. 


For every game, you will have already been randomly assigned a pokemon type for your own Friend Safari. When you add someone as a friend on your 3DS it will add them to the Friend Safari where you can find wild pokemon in a patch of grass. Each person will have three pokemon assigned to their safari and each of those pokemon is chosen from a predefined pool of pokemon. For example, if you had a fire type safari, your first pokemon would be chosen from either Growlithe, Ponyta, Pansear or Magmar. The second would be chosen from Charmeleon, Slugma, Larvesta or Pyroar and the third would be chosen from Ninetales, Braixen or Fletchinder. Each ‘slot’ is assigned a pokemon from each pool so you couldn’t have three pokemon from the same slot (e.g. Ponyta, Magmar AND Growlithe). This obviously leads to a large number of combinations of pokemon meaning that you’ll need to befriend a lot of people to get the right ones. Additionally, the friend needs to have completed the game (become the Champion) AND connected online at the same time as you to unlock the third slot. 


I would recommend going on a forum or message board (Gamefaqs usually has friend requests) to get as many of these as you can. There may be a time in the future where this system does not work due to the online services being shut down so this option may not be available in the future.
